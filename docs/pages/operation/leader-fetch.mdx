# Leader fetch

Every eRPC replica follows the chain head on its own: each one asks your providers for the
latest block about once per block. With ten replicas, that's ten head fetches per block.
Leader fetch lets one replica per network do it for the whole fleet and share each head with
the others through shared state, so the head-fetch bill stays flat as you scale out. If that
replica dies, another takes over within seconds, and in the meantime every replica goes back
to fetching on its own.

It is **off by default** and needs a shared-state connector that every replica reaches
(Redis).

### How it works

Each network with `evm.chainTracker.leaderFetch.enabled: true` runs one goroutine per
replica (`leaderFetch.run`, `erpc/network_leader_fetch.go`). It subscribes to the
network's channel on the shared-state connector and, every `leaseTtl / 3`, either renews
the lease it holds or tries to take it (`SET NX PX` of a random token).

- **Leader** (lease holder): its chain tracker's block clock runs exactly as without the
  feature (spec §6.8). Every header that one of its own fetches of an upstream's `latest`
  brought, and that its intake passed (integrity, chain identity), is published on the
  channel with the upstream id and the lease's **fencing epoch**.
- **Follower**: takes each published header through its **own intake** as that upstream's
  vote, as kind `HeaderLeader`: integrity checks, chain-identity gating, head counter and
  hash vote all run locally, and the tracker links it by parent hash like any other vote.
  To the block clock it arrives as a push, and it meets the upstream's own poll's latest
  part as a push does. While the newest accepted leader header is younger than
  `staleAfter = max(3 × block time, minStaleAfter)`, the follower's block clock fetches
  nothing (`ChainTracker.followsLeader`); it looks again when that time passes.
- **Demand**: a follower whose clock has demand (reads of latest, taps) sends a demand
  message at most every W/2; the leader marks its lanes read on it, so its clock runs for
  readers it does not serve itself.
- **Fencing**: each acquisition increments a never-expiring epoch counter in the same
  script that takes the lease. Followers drop headers whose epoch is below the highest they
  saw; a leader that sees a higher epoch stands down.

Cost: the cluster's `latest` fetches are about one leader's, ~1 per block plus the clock's
budget (spec §6.8), whatever the replica count. Upstreams' full polls (finalized, syncing)
still run on every replica at `statePollerInterval`.

### Trust tradeoff

The chain tracker's rule is that a hash vote comes only from this instance's own fetches
(spec §6.3). Leader fetch is an explicit opt-in relaxation: a follower's votes come from
another replica's fetches, relayed through shared state. What still holds: every relayed
header passes the follower's own integrity and chain-identity checks, its parent-hash
linkage decides the pins, and a deposed leader's late headers are fenced. What you trust
additionally: that the replica holding the lease is one of yours and your shared state is
not writable by others.

### Config schema

| Field | Type | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `evm.chainTracker.leaderFetch` | object | unset (off) | Unset or `enabled: false`: every replica runs its own clock, no behaviour change. |
| `leaderFetch.enabled` | bool | `false` | Needs a shared-state connector with leases and fan-out (`redis`; `memory` works within one process). Any other connector logs a warning and the network runs its own clock. |
| `leaderFetch.leaseTtl` | Duration | `3s` ([`DefaultLeaderFetchLeaseTtl`](https://github.com/erpc/erpc/blob/main/common/defaults.go#L2736)) | A dead leader is replaced within it plus one pass (`leaseTtl / 3`). Minimum `100ms`. |
| `leaderFetch.minStaleAfter` | Duration | `5s` ([`DefaultLeaderFetchMinStaleAfter`](https://github.com/erpc/erpc/blob/main/common/defaults.go#L2740)) | Floor of a follower's wait for the leader's next head before its own clock runs: `max(3 × block time, minStaleAfter)`. |

### Edge cases & gotchas

1. **Leader death**: a crashed leader stops renewing; its lease expires after `leaseTtl` and
   the next pass of another replica takes it (tested: `TestLeaderFetch_LeaderDeathHandsOver`,
   600ms handover at `leaseTtl: 600ms`). A graceful shutdown releases the lease at once.
2. **Silent leader** (lease held, headers lost): followers run their own clocks once the
   last header is `staleAfter` old (`TestLeaderFetch_StaleLeaderFallsBack`).
3. **Renew error** (shared state unreachable): the leader stands down rather than risk two
   leaders; followers fall back after `staleAfter`.
4. **Rejected header** (integrity, chain identity, unknown upstream id): counted, not voted,
   and does not refresh `staleAfter`, so a leader whose headers keep failing a follower's
   intake leaves it on its own clock.
5. **Push sources** (`ws`/`wss` with `newHeads`) still push to every replica; leader fetch
   only replaces the block clock's polling.
6. **Disabled** (`TestLeaderFetch_DisabledEveryInstanceFetches`): no goroutine, no channel,
   no lease; the clock's code path is unchanged.

### Observability

- `erpc_leader_fetch_role{project,network}`: 1 while this replica leads, else 0. Sum across
  replicas should be 1.
- `erpc_leader_fetch_transitions_total{project,network,event}`: `acquired`, `lost`.
- `erpc_leader_fetch_heads_total{project,network,outcome}`: leader `published`,
  `publish_failed`; follower `accepted`, `rejected`, `fenced`, `malformed`,
  `unknown_upstream`.
- `erpc_head_clock_total` on followers stays near zero while they follow.
